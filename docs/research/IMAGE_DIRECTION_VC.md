# Proposal B — MedTech Venture Photography Direction

Last updated: 2026-08-17

## Purpose

This is the active image-generation and editing direction for Proposal B (`/b`). It makes AMED's venture role visible through founder judgment, device diligence, clinical evidence, commercialization, and patient impact. The current approved treatment uses luminous white daylight, deep navy clothing, cool pale-grey surfaces, and crisp natural light-and-shadow contrast.

The active files are the explicit home and page assets listed below. Earlier originals, `home-warm-*`, `page-*-warm.png`, `page-*-editorial.png`, and `*-vc.png` alternatives are intentionally preserved as reversible fallbacks.

## Global prompt

Create premium photorealistic editorial photography for a global MedTech venture-capital website. Use strong but believable natural window light, luminous neutral white, deep navy, cool pale grey, realistic skin and hands, credible materials, and subtle 35mm documentary grain. Show founders presenting tangible medical technology while investors evaluate clinical evidence, commercialization, and risk. When East Asian people appear, they represent AMED's capital/investment side: they observe, question, compare evidence, or take notes rather than demonstrate the product. Preserve quiet composition room for oversized white typography. The mood is intelligent, calm, selective, global, and human — never glossy hospital stock photography or a perfect showroom render.

Avoid readable text, logos, brand marks, watermarks, money, handshakes, trophies, sci-fi holograms, neon cyberpunk interfaces, generic laboratory portraits, unsafe clinical procedures, distorted anatomy, impossible devices, waxy skin, mannequin poses, duplicated props, perfect symmetry, and excessive golden haze.

## Composition rules

- Hero and page-hero subjects should sit away from the navigation and leave a quiet band for large category typography.
- Devices must look tangible and mechanically plausible; use screens and documents as supporting evidence, not as the main subject.
- Venture capital should be communicated through informed evaluation, company-building, and evidence review rather than visual finance symbols.
- Maintain age, gender, and geographic diversity without turning the scene into a staged group portrait.
- The home grid must remain exactly seven subjects: venture capital ×2, medical ×1, neural macro ×1, device/surgery lab ×1, cardiovascular ×1, and biomedical macro ×1.
- The five section-page heroes form one backlit editorial campaign but must not repeat the same meeting composition. About uses luminous blue-white ribbed glass and a partnership exchange; Portfolio uses a steel-blue solitary transparent-heart study; Story uses lavender and a physical milestone sequence; Team uses warm ivory backlight and only the approved right-side walking pair; Contact uses mauve and a first pitch exchange. East Asian figures remain on the capital side in founder/investor scenes. Keep earlier assets intact and write future iterations to new filenames.

## Team portrait treatment

Team portraits come from the high-resolution `*-unified.png` sources in the prior project and use warm cream/taupe replacement backgrounds. Preserve exact identity, age, ethnicity, face geometry, expression, hair, glasses, clothing, and body proportions. Michelle Tsai and Jeremy Tseng use their approved `*-warm.png` files without further retouching. Most other active portraits use `*-warm-soft.png`; Fred Shen uses the more polished `*-warm-soft-v2.png` variant. Michael Wang, William Tai, and Joe Liu use non-destructive `*-jaw.png` variants that add only a restrained, soft under-chin shadow to separate face and neck. All retouching is limited to restrained lighting and reduction of harsh pore/microtexture; do not de-age, reshape, whiten, blur, add makeup, or create plastic skin.
