# AMED Ventures — Landing Page Copy Deck

**Creative direction.** AMED's own site carries roughly 80 words total. The brief explicitly
asks us to supply Creative Writing / Creative Direction / Copywriting on top of the source
material, so everything below is written for AMED and inferred from three verified facts:

1. Verbatim from amedventures.com — "AMED Ventures, a venture capital based in the San Francisco
   Bay Area, believes in funding exceptional entrepreneurs and MedTech innovations that make a
   real impact on our lives."
2. Verbatim from amedventures.com — the team has "extensive experience and a documented history
   in investment, business development, and operational management spanning large multinational
   corporations to early-stage companies," and provides value "beyond capital provision."
3. The 15 named portfolio companies (below), which are overwhelmingly interventional —
   neurovascular, cardiovascular, and structural-heart device companies.

**Voice.** Institutional but not corporate. Confident without hype. Plain words, long view,
clinical precision. Never "revolutionary," never "disruptive," never exclamation marks.
The register the brief asks for: bright, elegant, humane, institutional.

**Positioning line (internal, not printed):** AMED backs the people who build the instruments
medicine will use next.

**Step 01 — Intervention over observation**

## Section 07 — Approach (sticky title + accordion)

**Eyebrow:** ( APPROACH ) 06
**Title:** How we partner.

1. **What stage do you invest at?**
   Primarily seed and Series A, where a device has a defined clinical problem and a credible
   path through regulation. We hold reserves to keep supporting companies well past the first
   round.

2. **What do you look for in a founder?**
   Direct experience shipping a regulated product, or a clinical insight so specific it could
   only have come from practice. We would rather back a narrow problem understood deeply than
   a broad one understood loosely.

3. **Which areas are you focused on?**
   Interventional medical devices — neurovascular, cardiovascular, and structural heart — plus
   adjacent technologies that make those procedures safer, faster, or available to more people.

4. **How involved are you after investing?**
   As involved as the company needs and no more. In practice that usually means regulatory and
   clinical strategy, help hiring the first quality and manufacturing leaders, and
   introductions to clinicians and strategics.

5. **How should I get in touch?**
   Send the company overview and the clinical problem you are solving. If it is close to what
   we do, you will hear back.
